Why we use pescada amarela

In Britain, fish and chips usually means cod or haddock. Both are cold-water North Atlantic fish. The cod sold in Brazil commonly arrives salted and dried, with a different texture from fresh fish in batter.

We use pescada amarela, or acoupa weakfish, caught off the coast of Ceará. It is white, firm and mild, with large flakes that hold together in the fryer.

This local fish gives the recipe the fresh texture we want.

A London plate on a tropical coast

Battered fried fish reached England with Jewish immigrants from Portugal and Spain. Fried potatoes became street food in the industrial north. The two foods were sold together by around 1860, and shops in London's East End and Lancashire both claim the first serving.

The dish became popular among workers and was sold hot in newspaper. The British government kept it outside food rationing during the Second World War. The printed paper under our plate refers to that history.

Come for the late afternoon

Fortaleza sits three degrees below the equator, so the sun sets between 5:25 pm and 5:56 pm throughout the year. The light starts turning about forty minutes earlier.

The tables face the water. Arrive around 4:00 pm or book ahead for a better chance of sitting in the front row.

Questions we actually get

Do you serve rollmops?

No. We use the British influence in fried dishes made with local ingredients, including fish and chips and panko prawns.

Is there anything for someone who doesn't eat seafood?

Yes. Meat and poultry are available all day, and five of the seven weekday set lunches use those proteins.

Is English spoken?

Service is mainly in Portuguese. The menu has photographs, and you can message us on WhatsApp before you come.

Can I eat alone?

Yes. A table for one can be booked online, and the counter also seats individual guests when space is available.
